Our paper examines how the Russia–Ukraine war changed perceptions of country risk in 47 countries. Concerns about risk were already rising before the invasion, so some escalation was expected. Before the war, countries with more financial weaknesses appeared riskier. **After the invasion, especially as it became clear the conflict would continue, geopolitical factors mattered more.** Countries that were farther from Moscow and those in NATO were generally seen as more resilient. We argue that these reactions differed from those in stock markets, showing that the two markets capture different aspects of geopolitical risk.
