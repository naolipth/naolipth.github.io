This study provides a comprehensive evaluation of stock return volatility forecasting using various GARCH-type models, analyzing their performance across the entire, pre-, during, and post-pandemic periods. We assess model performance using both the outperformance testing based on average results and winning ratios based on the best-performed frequency. Our overall findings reveal that **no single model consistently outperforms others across all periods and forecasting horizons**. Furthermore, we find that **using winning frequency versus significance of outperformance leads to different conclusions**. In terms of winning frequency, GARCH(1,1) consistently performs better than asymmetric GARCH models across both short-run and long-run horizons, particularly in the pre- and post-pandemic periods. However, based on the testing results, asymmetric GARCH models exhibit significant outperformance in pre- and post-pandemic periods.
